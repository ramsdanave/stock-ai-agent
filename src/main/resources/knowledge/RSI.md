# Relative Strength Index (RSI)

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum oscillator used in
technical analysis to measure the speed and magnitude of recent price
movements.

RSI normally ranges from 0 to 100.

An RSI above 70 is commonly considered an overbought condition.

An RSI below 30 is commonly considered an oversold condition.

An RSI around 50 represents a more neutral momentum condition.

RSI should not be interpreted in isolation. Traders and analysts may
combine RSI with price trends, moving averages, MACD, volume and other
technical indicators.

A low RSI does not guarantee that a stock price will increase.

A high RSI does not guarantee that a stock price will decrease.

RSI is calculated from average gains and average losses over a specified
period. RSI 14 refers to the commonly used 14-period calculation.
